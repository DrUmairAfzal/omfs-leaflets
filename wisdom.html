In short

  • We usually take out a wisdom tooth only if it causes problems, or is likely to.
  • Expect swelling and a stiff jaw. It is often worst 2 to 3 days after the operation.
  • If the tooth is close to the nerve, there is a small risk of a numb lip or chin. This usually gets better.
  • Most people need a few days off work or school.

You have been offered an operation to take out a wisdom tooth. This leaflet explains why, what happens, your choices and the risks.

Please read it before your appointment. Write down any questions and bring them with you.

Why does my wisdom tooth need to come out?

Wisdom teeth are the last teeth at the back of the mouth. They often come through between the ages of 17 and 24. Many get stuck because there is not enough room.

A stuck wisdom tooth does not always need to come out. We usually advise taking it out if it causes problems, or is likely to harm the tooth in front. Problems include:

  • gum infections around the tooth that keep coming back
  • decay (holes) in the wisdom tooth or the tooth in front of it
  • gum disease around the tooth in front
  • a cyst, which is a bag of fluid in the jaw (this is rare)

What are my choices?

  1. Leave the tooth alone. Your dentist can keep an eye on it. If it keeps causing infections or pain, these may carry on or get worse.
  2. Take out the whole tooth. This is what this leaflet is about.
  3. Coronectomy. If the roots are very close to the , we can take out only the top of the tooth and leave the roots. There is a separate leaflet about this.

The choice is yours. You can change your mind at any time, even after you have signed the form to say yes.

Before your appointment

  • Tell us about all the medicines you take. This includes ones you buy from a shop.
  • Tell us if you take a medicine to thin your blood, such as warfarin, apixaban or rivaroxaban.
  • Tell us if you take a medicine for your bones, such as alendronic acid or denosumab. Tell us even if you have stopped it.
  • Keep taking your medicines unless we tell you to stop.
  • If you are having or a , arrange for an adult to take you home. Your letter will tell you if you need to stop eating and drinking.
  • Plan a few days off work or school.
  • Have some soft food at home.
  • Have paracetamol or ibuprofen at home, if they are safe for you.
  • Have an ice pack, or a bag of frozen peas, ready to wrap in a towel.

What happens on the day?

There are three ways to have the operation. Your surgeon will talk to you about which is best.

  • Awake, with a numbing injection. This is called a .
  • Awake but relaxed. You have a numbing injection, and medicine into a vein to make you calm and sleepy. This is called sedation.
  • Asleep. Medicine puts you fully to sleep. This is called a general anaesthetic. Most people still go home the same day.
  1. We open the gum over the tooth.
  2. We remove a little bone around the tooth, if needed.
  3. We often cut the tooth into pieces. This means we can take it out through a smaller gap.
  4. We close the gum with . These melt away by themselves in about 2 weeks.

You should not feel pain. You will feel pushing and pressure, and you may hear the drill.

After the operation

  1. On the day
    • Your mouth is numb for a few hours.
    • Bite on gauze if it bleeds.
    • Do not rinse your mouth.
  2. Days 2 to 3
    • Swelling and bruising are often at their worst.
    • Your jaw may feel stiff.
  3. Up to 2 weeks
    • The swelling settles.
    • The stitches melt away.
  • Your mouth will be sore for a few days. Take paracetamol or ibuprofen, if they are safe for you. Follow the instructions on the packet.
  • Your jaw may be stiff, so it may be hard to open wide. This gets better as the swelling goes down.
  • Most people need a few days off work or school.
  • After sedation, do not drive for 24 hours. After a general anaesthetic, do not drive for 48 hours.

Looking after your mouth

  • On the day, do not rinse your mouth. Rinsing can start the bleeding again.
  • On the first day, hold an ice pack wrapped in a towel on your cheek, on and off. This helps the swelling.
  • From the next day, rinse gently with warm salt water after meals, for about a week. Use 1 teaspoon of salt in a cup of warm water.
  • Keep brushing your teeth. Be gentle near the wound.
  • Eat soft food for a few days. Wait until the numbness has gone before you have hot drinks.
  • Try not to smoke for at least a few days. Smoking slows healing and makes infection more likely.
  • If it bleeds, roll up a clean, damp cloth or piece of gauze. Bite on it firmly for 20 minutes. Do not keep checking it.

What are the risks?

Your surgeon will look at your X-rays and talk to you about your own risks. This list follows your consent form. Numbers come from national guidance or studies. Common means more than 1 in 20 people, Less common fewer than 1 in 20 and Rare fewer than 1 in 100.

During or just after the operation

The tooth breaks as it comes outCommon
Numb or tingly lower lip and chin, if the tooth is close to the nerveUp to 9 in 100This is usually temporary. Fewer than 1 in 100 people are left with some numbness for life.
Numb or tingly lower lip and chin, if the tooth is well away from the nerveLess than 1 in 100
Numb or tingly tongue, or a change in tasteLess than 1 in 100
Damage to the tooth in front, your lips or gumsLess common
We cannot finish the operation as planned, or a broken jawLess than 1 in 100

In the days after

PainMost people
Swelling and a stiff jawCommon
A numb or tingly feeling near the areaCommon
Dry socket: the hole left in the gum becomes very painful and is slow to healAbout 2 in 100More likely in people who smoke.
Bleeding at home, or a wound that is slow to heal or gets infectedLess common

Months or years later

The jaw bone does not healLess commonThis mainly affects people who take some bone medicines or have had radiotherapy to the jaw. Tell us if this is you.
Infection in the jaw boneRare

Nerve damage most often gets better, but this can take up to 18 months.

When should I get help?

☎Phone us if:

  • bleeding does not stop after biting on gauze for 20 minutes, twice
  • pain or swelling gets worse after the third day
  • you feel hot and unwell, or the wound tastes or smells bad
  • your lip, chin or tongue is still numb the day after the operation

!Go to A&E or call 999 if:

  • you find it hard to breathe or swallow
  • swelling spreads to your eye or down your neck
  • bleeding is heavy and will not stop
