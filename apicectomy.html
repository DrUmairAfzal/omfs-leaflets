In short

  • Infection is still there at the tip of a root-filled tooth. We remove the tip and seal the root.
  • It is often the last chance to save the tooth.
  • It takes up to an hour, with a numbing injection. Swelling is worst on day 2 or 3.
  • Bone heals slowly. Your dentist may check it with an X-ray after a few months.

You have been offered an apicectomy. This leaflet explains what it is, your choices and the risks.

Please read it before your appointment. Write down any questions and bring them with you.

What is an apicectomy?

Your tooth has had , but infection is still there at the tip of the root. This tip is called the apex.

In an apicectomy, we remove the tip of the root and the infected tissue around it. We then seal the end of the root with a small filling. This gives the bone a chance to heal.

It is often the last chance to save the tooth.

What are my choices?

  1. Redo the root canal treatment. This is often tried first. You may be referred to a specialist in root canal treatment (an endodontist).
  2. Take the tooth out. This removes the infection. You may then want a denture, bridge or implant to fill the gap.
  3. Apicectomy. We treat the infection through the gum and try to keep the tooth.
  4. Leave things as they are. The infection may get worse, and the tooth may need to come out later.

The choice is yours. You can change your mind at any time, even after you have signed the .

Before your appointment

  • Tell us about all the medicines you take. This means ones from your doctor and ones you buy.
  • Tell us if you take a , such as warfarin, apixaban or rivaroxaban.
  • Tell us if you take, or have ever taken, a medicine for your bones, such as alendronic acid or denosumab.
  • Keep taking your medicines unless we tell you to stop.
  • You can eat and drink as normal before an apicectomy under .

What happens on the day?

Most people have an apicectomy awake, with an injection to numb the area.

  1. We lift the gum away from the bone above the root.
  2. We remove a little bone to reach the tip of the root.
  3. We remove the infected tissue and about 3 mm from the end of the root.
  4. We seal the end of the root with a small filling.
  5. We put the gum back with . Some melt away. Others are taken out after about a week.

It usually takes 45 minutes to an hour. You should not feel pain, but you will feel pushing.

After the operation

  • Your mouth will be sore for a few days. Take painkillers such as paracetamol or ibuprofen, if they are safe for you. Do what the packet says.
  • Swelling and bruising are normal. They are often worst on the second or third day.
  • The tooth may feel tender or a little loose for a short time.
  • Most people need a day or two off work.

Looking after your mouth

  • Do not rinse your mouth on the day of the operation. Rinsing can start the bleeding again.
  • For the first day, hold an ice pack wrapped in a towel against your cheek, on and off, to help the swelling.
  • From the next day, rinse gently with warm salt water after meals, for about a week. Use 1 teaspoon of salt in a cup of warm water.
  • Keep brushing your teeth. Be gentle near the wound.
  • Eat soft food for a few days. Do not have hot drinks until the numbness has worn off.
  • Try not to smoke for at least the first few days. Smoking slows healing and makes infection more likely.
  • If it bleeds, roll up a clean, damp handkerchief or piece of gauze. Bite on it firmly for at least 20 minutes without checking.

When should I get help?

☎Phone us if:

  • bleeding has not stopped after you have pressed on it for 20 minutes, twice
  • pain or swelling gets worse after the third day
  • you feel hot and unwell, or there is a bad taste or smell from the wound

!Go to A&E or call 999 if:

  • you find it hard to breathe or swallow
  • swelling spreads to your eye or down your neck
  • bleeding is heavy and will not stop

Will I need to come back?

If you have stitches that need taking out, we will see you after about a week.

Your dentist may take an X-ray after a few months to check the bone is healing. Bone heals slowly, so this can take up to a year.
