In short

  • Most teeth come out in one piece. Some need a small operation with stitches.
  • Bite on gauze and do not rinse on the first day, so a blood clot can form.
  • From the next day, rinse gently with warm salt water.
  • Phone us if the pain gets worse 2 to 4 days later. This may be a dry socket.

You have been offered treatment to take out a tooth. This leaflet explains what happens, your choices, the risks, and how to look after your mouth afterwards.

Please read it before your appointment. Write down any questions and bring them with you.

What happens when a tooth is taken out?

Most teeth can be eased out of the in one piece. Some need a small operation. This might be because the tooth is broken, buried, or has curved roots.

For a small operation, we open the gum, may remove a little bone, and may cut the tooth into pieces. We then close the gum with .

Afterwards, a blood clot forms in the socket. This is the start of healing. Over the next few weeks, gum and new bone fill the space.

What are my choices?

  1. Keep the tooth. Some teeth can be saved with a filling, or a crown. Ask your dentist if this is possible for you.
  2. Leave things as they are. If the tooth is infected or painful, this may get worse. Infection from a tooth can spread.
  3. Take the tooth out. This is what this leaflet is about.

Think about how you would like to fill the gap, if at all. Options include a denture, a bridge or an implant. Your dentist can talk these through with you.

The choice is yours. You can change your mind at any time, even after you have signed the .

Before your appointment

  • Tell us about all the medicines you take. This means ones from your doctor and ones you buy.
  • Tell us if you take a , such as warfarin, apixaban or rivaroxaban.
  • Tell us if you take, or have ever taken, a medicine for your bones, such as alendronic acid or denosumab.
  • Keep taking your medicines unless we tell you to stop.
  • If you are having a , eat a light meal a couple of hours before your appointment.
  • If you are having or a , your letter will tell you if you need to stop eating and drinking. You will need an adult to take you home.

What happens on the day?

  1. We numb the area with an injection. This can make your heart beat faster for a few minutes. This is normal.
  2. We loosen the tooth and take it out. You should not feel pain, but you will feel pushing and pressure.
  3. If you need stitches, they melt away by themselves in about 2 weeks.
  4. You bite on a piece of gauze to help a clot form. We check the bleeding has stopped before you leave.

Some people have sedation or a general anaesthetic instead. Your surgeon will talk to you about this if it suits you.

After your tooth is taken out

  • The numbness wears off after a few hours. Take care not to bite your lip, cheek or tongue until then.
  • Your mouth will be sore for a few days. Take painkillers such as paracetamol or ibuprofen, if they are safe for you. Do what the packet says.
  • Most people can go back to work or school the next day.
  • Do not drink alcohol or do hard exercise for 24 hours.

Looking after your mouth

  • Do not rinse your mouth on the day of the operation. Rinsing can start the bleeding again.
  • From the next day, rinse gently with warm salt water after meals, for about a week. Use 1 teaspoon of salt in a cup of warm water.
  • Keep brushing your teeth. Be gentle near the wound.
  • Eat soft food for a few days. Do not have hot drinks until the numbness has worn off.
  • Try not to smoke for at least the first few days. Smoking slows healing and makes infection more likely.
  • If it bleeds, roll up a clean, damp handkerchief or piece of gauze. Bite on it firmly for at least 20 minutes without checking.

What are the risks?

Every operation has some risk. Your risks depend on which tooth is coming out. Your surgeon will talk to you about your own risks.

Dry socket (a very painful socket that is slow to heal)1 to 4More likely after lower back teeth and in people who smoke. It starts 2 to 4 days after the tooth comes out. We can put a soothing dressing in the socket.

When should I get help?

☎Phone us if:

  • bleeding has not stopped after you have pressed on it for 20 minutes, twice
  • the pain gets worse instead of better, often 2 to 4 days later. This may be a
  • you feel hot and unwell, or there is a bad taste or smell from the wound
  • upper back tooth only: drinks come out of your nose, or you can feel air passing between your mouth and nose

!Go to A&E or call 999 if:

  • you find it hard to breathe or swallow
  • swelling spreads to your eye or down your neck
  • bleeding is heavy and will not stop
