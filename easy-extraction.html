About this leaflet

This leaflet is about having a tooth taken out.

You can ask someone to read it with you.

Why your tooth needs to come out

Your tooth may be broken, painful or infected.

We have talked with you about taking it out.

It is your choice

You can say yes or no.

You can change your mind at any time.

Before you come

Tell us about all the medicines you take.

This includes medicines that thin your blood.

Eat a small meal before you come.

On the day

We give you an injection in your mouth.

Your mouth goes numb.

Numb means you cannot feel pain there.

You will feel pushing.

It should not hurt.

We take your tooth out.

Sometimes we use stitches.

Stitches are small threads that hold the gum together.

They melt away by themselves.

Straight after

Bite on the gauze.

Gauze is a small soft pad.

It helps the bleeding stop.

Do not rinse your mouth today.

Your mouth will be numb for a few hours.

Be careful not to bite your lip or tongue.

Do not have hot drinks until the numbness goes.

The next few days

Your mouth may be sore for a few days.

This is normal.

You can take painkillers like paracetamol.

Eat soft food for a few days.

From tomorrow, rinse with warm salt water after meals.

Keep brushing your teeth gently.

Try not to smoke.

Smoking slows down healing.

If something is wrong

If it bleeds, bite on clean gauze.

Bite hard for 20 minutes without looking.

Sometimes the gap gets very painful 2 to 4 days later.

This is called a dry socket.

We can help with this.

Phone us if:

  • it keeps bleeding
  • the pain gets worse
  • you feel hot and unwell.

Call 999 if:

  • you find it hard to breathe or swallow
  • your face or neck swells a lot.

Your questions

Write down any questions you have.

Bring them to your appointment.

Contact us

Contact the Maxillofacial Unit. The phone numbers are on your printed leaflet and your appointment letter.

If you cannot reach us, call NHS 111. In an emergency, call 999.

You can ask for this leaflet in large print, in another language, or read to you.
