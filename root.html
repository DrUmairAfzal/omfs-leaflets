In short

  • A piece of root moved into your sinus while an upper tooth was being taken out.
  • We usually advise taking it out, to prevent sinus infections.
  • It is often done under a general anaesthetic, through the socket or a small opening inside the mouth.
  • For at least 2 weeks, do not blow your nose, use a straw, smoke or fly.

While an upper back tooth was being taken out, a piece of root moved into your sinus. This leaflet explains what this means, your choices and the risks.

What has happened?

Your is an air space in your cheek bone. The roots of your upper back teeth sit just below it. Sometimes only a very thin layer of bone, or none, separates them.

When one of these teeth is taken out, a root or piece of tooth can slip up into the sinus. This happens in about 1 to 4 of every 100 upper back teeth taken out. It is a known risk and does not mean something was done wrong.

Why does it need to come out?

A piece of tooth in the sinus can cause sinus infections. It can also stop the hole between your mouth and sinus from healing. So we usually advise taking it out, ideally soon.

What are my choices?

  1. Take the root out. This is what we usually advise.
  2. Leave it and keep an eye on it. This is sometimes possible for a very small piece with no infection. It may cause sinus problems later.

The choice is yours. You can change your mind at any time, even after you have signed the .

Before your operation

  • You will usually have a scan to show exactly where the root is.
  • You may be given and a nose spray.
  • Tell us about all the medicines you take, and keep taking them unless we tell you to stop.
  • If you are having a , your letter will tell you when to stop eating and drinking. You will need an adult to take you home.

Until your operation, follow the advice in 'Protecting your sinus' below.

What happens on the day?

This is often done under a general anaesthetic. We may be able to take the root out:

  • Through the tooth socket. This works if the root is near the hole.
  • Through a small opening in the front wall of the sinus. We make this from inside your mouth, above your teeth, so there is no cut on your face.
  • With a camera through the nose. This is done by an ear, nose and throat surgeon.

We then close the hole between your mouth and sinus with .

After the operation

  • Your mouth will be sore for a few days. Take painkillers such as paracetamol or ibuprofen, if they are safe for you.
  • Your cheek will swell and may bruise. It is often worst on the third day.
  • A little bleeding from your nose on the same side is normal for a day or two.
  • Eat soft food and chew on the other side for 2 weeks.
  • Most people need about a week off work.
  • We will usually see you 1 to 2 weeks after the operation to check you are healing.

You will usually be given these medicines:

  • Antibiotics, usually for 5 to 7 days, to help stop the sinus getting infected. Finish the whole course.
  • A nose spray or drops, to keep the sinus clear. Use it in the nostril on the same side as the operation, as the label says, for no more than 7 days. Tell us if you have heart problems, high blood pressure or diabetes, or take medicine for depression.
  • Steam, to help the sinus drain. Breathe in steam from a bowl of hot (not boiling) water for 5 minutes, 3 or 4 times a day. Keep children away from the bowl.

Protecting your sinus for 2 weeks

  • Do not blow your nose. Wipe it gently instead.
  • If you need to sneeze, keep your mouth open.
  • Do not drink through a straw or suck on a sports bottle.
  • Do not smoke or vape, blow up balloons, or play a wind or brass instrument.
  • Do not swim, dive or fly until we tell you it is safe. For flying and diving, this can be up to 6 weeks.
  • Avoid heavy lifting and hard exercise for the first week.

What are the risks?

Every operation has some risk. Your risks depend on how the root is taken out. Your surgeon will talk to you about your own risks.

When should I get help?

☎Phone us if:

  • bleeding has not stopped after you have pressed on it for 20 minutes, twice
  • pain or swelling gets worse after the third day
  • you feel hot and unwell, or there is a bad taste or smell from the wound
  • drinks come out of your nose, or you can feel air passing between your mouth and nose
  • you have a blocked nose with a bad smell, or pain in your cheek that is getting worse

!Go to A&E or call 999 if:

  • you find it hard to breathe or swallow
  • swelling spreads to your eye or down your neck
  • bleeding is heavy and will not stop
