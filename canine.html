Uncovering a buried canine tooth

Surgery to help your brace bring an eye tooth into place

You have been offered an operation to uncover a buried canine tooth. This leaflet explains what happens, your choices and the risks.

If you are a parent or carer, please read it with your child. Bring any questions to your appointment.

What is a buried canine?

Canines are the pointed 'eye teeth' next to your front teeth. Upper canines come through at about 11 to 13 years old.

Sometimes a canine grows in the wrong place and gets stuck under the gum, often in the roof of the mouth. This happens to about 2 or 3 in every 100 young people.

Why does it need treatment?

Canines have long, strong roots. They are important for biting and for your smile.

A buried canine can press on the roots of the front teeth next to it and damage them. Rarely, a cyst (a sac of fluid) can form around it.

What are my choices?

  1. Leave the tooth where it is. Some buried canines stay put for life without causing trouble. You may need X-rays to check it is not harming the teeth next to it.
  2. Take the canine out. Your brace may then close the gap, or the gap may be filled later.
  3. Uncover the tooth and move it into place with a brace. This is what this leaflet is about. Brace treatment usually takes 2 to 3 years in total.

Your orthodontist and surgeon will plan your treatment together. The choice is yours. You can change your mind at any time, even after you have signed the consent form.

Before your appointment

  • Your orthodontist may fit your brace before the operation. They will tell you.
  • Tell your orthodontist the date of your operation, so they can see you about 2 weeks afterwards.
  • Tell us about all the medicines you take, and keep taking them unless we tell you to stop.
  • If you are having a general anaesthetic, your letter will tell you when to stop eating and drinking. An adult will need to take you home.

What happens on the day?

Many young people have this done asleep, under a general anaesthetic, and go home the same day. Some have it awake, with an injection to numb the area.

We lift the gum and remove a little bone to uncover the tooth. Then we do one of these. Your surgeon will have talked to you about which one.

  • A gold chain (closed exposure). We glue a small bracket with a thin gold chain onto the tooth. We put the gum back, and the end of the chain comes out through the gum. Your orthodontist uses it to pull the tooth down slowly.
  • A window in the gum (open exposure). We remove a small piece of gum so the tooth can be seen. We may cover it with a dressing (a pack) held by stitches. We take this out after about 10 days to 2 weeks.

The tooth does not move during the operation. It moves slowly after the operation, with your brace.

After the operation

  • Your mouth will be sore for a few days, but there is not often much swelling. Paracetamol or ibuprofen will help, if they are safe for you.
  • Most stitches melt away by themselves in about 2 weeks.
  • You will probably need a day or two off school or work.
  • Try not to touch or pull the chain with your tongue or fingers.
  • Your orthodontist will start moving the tooth about 2 weeks after the operation, once the gum has healed.

Looking after your mouth

  • Do not rinse your mouth on the day of the operation. Rinsing can start the bleeding again.
  • From the next day, rinse gently with warm salt water after meals, for about a week. Use 1 teaspoon of salt in a cup of warm water.
  • Keep brushing your teeth. Be gentle near the wound.
  • Eat soft food for a few days. Do not have hot drinks until the numbness has worn off.
  • Try not to smoke for at least the first few days. Smoking slows healing and makes infection more likely.
  • If it bleeds, roll up a clean, damp handkerchief or piece of gauze. Bite on it firmly for at least 20 minutes without checking.

Will I need to come back?

You will usually see the surgeon or your orthodontist about 2 weeks after the operation. After that, your orthodontist will see you often while your brace moves the tooth.

When should I get help?

Phone us if:

  • bleeding has not stopped after you have pressed on it for 20 minutes, twice
  • pain or swelling gets worse after the third day
  • you feel hot and unwell, or there is a bad taste or smell from the wound
  • the chain comes off or the pack falls out early. You can also contact your orthodontist.

Go to A&E or call 999 if:

  • you find it hard to breathe or swallow
  • swelling spreads to your eye or down your neck
  • bleeding is heavy and will not stop
