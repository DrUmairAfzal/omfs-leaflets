In short

  • A canine (eye tooth) is stuck under the gum. We uncover it so your brace can bring it into place.
  • Your mouth will be sore for a few days. Do not pull on the chain.
  • Your orthodontist starts moving the tooth about 2 weeks after the operation.
  • Brace treatment usually takes 2 to 3 years in total.

You have been offered an operation to uncover a buried canine tooth. This leaflet explains what happens, your choices and the risks.

If you are a parent or carer, please read it with your child. Bring any questions to your appointment.

What is a buried canine?

A canine is the pointed tooth next to your front teeth. Some people call it an eye tooth. Top canines usually come through at about 11 to 13 years old.

Sometimes a canine grows in the wrong place and gets stuck under the gum. This is often in the roof of the mouth. It happens to about 2 or 3 in every 100 young people.

Why does it need treatment?

Canines have long, strong roots. They are important for biting and for your smile.

A buried canine can press on the roots of the front teeth next to it and damage them. Rarely, a cyst (a bag of fluid) can form around it.

What are my choices?

  1. Leave the tooth where it is. Some buried canines stay put for life without causing trouble. You may need X-rays to check it is not harming the teeth next to it.
  2. Take the canine out. Your brace may then close the gap, or the gap may be filled later.
  3. Uncover the tooth and move it into place with a brace. This is what this leaflet is about. Brace treatment usually takes 2 to 3 years in total.

Your (brace specialist) and your surgeon will plan your treatment together. The choice is yours. You can change your mind at any time, even after you have signed the form to say yes.

Before your appointment

  • Your orthodontist may fit your brace before the operation. They will tell you.
  • Tell your orthodontist the date of your operation. They need to see you about 2 weeks afterwards.
  • Tell us about all the medicines you take. This includes ones you buy from a shop.
  • Tell us if you take a medicine to thin your blood, such as warfarin, apixaban or rivaroxaban.
  • Tell us if you take a medicine for your bones, such as alendronic acid or denosumab. Tell us even if you have stopped it.
  • Keep taking your medicines unless we tell you to stop.
  • If you are having a , your letter will tell you when to stop eating and drinking. An adult will need to take you home.
  • Have some soft food at home.
  • Have paracetamol or ibuprofen at home, if they are safe for you.

What happens on the day?

Many young people have this done asleep, under a general anaesthetic. They go home the same day. Some have it awake, with a numbing injection.

We lift the gum and remove a little bone to uncover the tooth. Then we do one of two things. Your surgeon will tell you which.

  • Fix a gold chain to the tooth. We glue a small button with a thin gold chain onto the tooth. We put the gum back. The end of the chain comes out through the gum. Your orthodontist uses it to pull the tooth down slowly.
  • Make a window in the gum. We remove a small piece of gum so the tooth can be seen. We may cover it with a soft dressing (a pack), held by . We take this out after about 10 days to 2 weeks.

The tooth does not move during the operation. It moves slowly afterwards, with your brace.

After the operation

  1. The first few days
    • Your mouth is sore.
    • There is not often much swelling.
    • Most people need a day or two off.
  2. About 2 weeks
    • Most stitches melt away.
    • Your orthodontist starts moving the tooth.
  3. 2 to 3 years
    • Your brace brings the tooth into place.
  • Paracetamol or ibuprofen will help the soreness, if they are safe for you.
  • Try not to touch or pull the chain with your tongue or fingers.

Looking after your mouth

  • On the day, do not rinse your mouth. Rinsing can start the bleeding again.
  • From the next day, rinse gently with warm salt water after meals, for about a week. Use 1 teaspoon of salt in a cup of warm water.
  • Keep brushing your teeth. Be gentle near the wound.
  • Eat soft food for a few days. Wait until the numbness has gone before you have hot drinks.
  • Try not to smoke for at least a few days. Smoking slows healing and makes infection more likely.
  • If it bleeds, roll up a clean, damp cloth or piece of gauze. Bite on it firmly for 20 minutes. Do not keep checking it.

What are the risks?

Your surgeon will talk to you about your own risks. This list follows your consent form. Numbers come from national guidance or studies. Common means more than 1 in 20 people, Less common fewer than 1 in 20 and Rare fewer than 1 in 100.

Will I need to come back?

You will usually see the surgeon or your orthodontist about 2 weeks after the operation. After that, your orthodontist will see you often while your brace moves the tooth.

When should I get help?

☎Phone us if:

  • bleeding does not stop after biting on gauze for 20 minutes, twice
  • pain or swelling gets worse after the third day
  • you feel hot and unwell, or the wound tastes or smells bad
  • the chain comes off or the pack falls out early. You can also contact your orthodontist.

!Go to A&E or call 999 if:

  • you find it hard to breathe or swallow
  • swelling spreads to your eye or down your neck
  • bleeding is heavy and will not stop
