In short

  • We take a small piece of tissue so an expert can find out what it is.
  • It takes about 15 minutes with a numbing injection. Most people go back to work the same day.
  • Most biopsies do not show cancer.
  • Your result usually comes in about 2 weeks. Phone us if you have not heard within 3 weeks.

You have been offered a biopsy. This leaflet explains what it involves, the risks, and how you will get your result.

Please read it before your appointment. Write down any questions and bring them with you.

What is a biopsy?

A biopsy is a small operation. We take a small piece of tissue from a patch, lump or sore in your mouth. An expert then looks at it under a microscope to find out what it is.

There are two main types:

  • Taking out the whole area. We do this for small lumps. You may not need any more treatment.
  • Taking out a small piece. This tells us what the area is, so we can plan the right treatment.

Why do I need a biopsy?

Looking at an area is not always enough to know what it is. A biopsy is the best way to find out.

Many people worry that a biopsy means cancer. Most biopsies show something that is not cancer. If you are worried, please tell us. We are happy to talk it through.

What are my choices?

  1. Have the biopsy. This is the best way to find out what the area is.
  2. Wait and check the area again later. This is only safe for some patches. If the area needs treatment, waiting could delay it. Your surgeon will tell you if waiting is an option for you.

The choice is yours. You can change your mind at any time, even after you have signed the .

Before your appointment

  • Tell us about all the medicines you take. This means ones from your doctor and ones you buy.
  • Tell us if you take a , such as warfarin, apixaban or rivaroxaban.
  • Tell us if you take, or have ever taken, a medicine for your bones, such as alendronic acid or denosumab.
  • Keep taking your medicines unless we tell you to stop.
  • You can eat and drink as normal before a biopsy under .
  • A few biopsies are done under a , such as when the area is hard to reach. If this applies to you, your letter will explain what to do.

What happens on the day?

  1. We numb the area with an injection. It takes a couple of minutes to work.
  2. We take the sample. You should not feel pain, but you may feel pushing.
  3. Most of the time, we close the area with . These melt away by themselves in about 2 weeks.

The whole thing takes about 15 minutes. Most people can go back to work or school the same day.

After the biopsy

  • The numbness wears off after a few hours. Be careful not to bite your lip, cheek or tongue until then.
  • Most people have only a little pain or swelling, for a few days. Paracetamol or ibuprofen can help, if they are safe for you.
  • A biopsy from the tongue is often more sore than one from other parts of the mouth.
  • It is normal for your spit to have a little blood in it on the first day.

Looking after your mouth

  • Do not rinse your mouth on the day of the operation. Rinsing can start the bleeding again.
  • From the next day, rinse gently with warm salt water after meals, for about a week. Use 1 teaspoon of salt in a cup of warm water.
  • Keep brushing your teeth. Be gentle near the wound.
  • Eat soft food for a few days. Do not have hot drinks until the numbness has worn off.
  • Try not to smoke for at least the first few days. Smoking slows healing and makes infection more likely.
  • If it bleeds, roll up a clean, damp handkerchief or piece of gauze. Bite on it firmly for at least 20 minutes without checking.

What are the risks?

A biopsy is a small operation and serious problems are rare. There are no good figures for each kind of mouth biopsy, so we have used words, not numbers.

Your results

We will give you your result, usually at an appointment about 2 weeks after the biopsy. If the result is urgent, we will contact you sooner.

Make sure you get your result

  • If you have not heard from us within 3 weeks, please phone us.
  • Do not assume that no news is good news.

When should I get help?

☎Phone us if:

  • bleeding has not stopped after you have pressed on it for 20 minutes, twice
  • pain or swelling gets worse after the third day
  • you feel hot and unwell, or there is a bad taste or smell from the wound

!Go to A&E or call 999 if:

  • you find it hard to breathe or swallow
  • swelling spreads to your eye or down your neck
  • bleeding is heavy and will not stop
