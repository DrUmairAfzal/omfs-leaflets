In short

  • We take a small piece from your mouth so an expert can check what it is.
  • It takes about 15 minutes, with a numbing injection.
  • Most biopsies do not show cancer.
  • Your result usually comes in about 2 weeks. Phone us if you have not heard within 3 weeks.

You have been offered a biopsy. This leaflet explains what happens, the risks, and how you will get your result.

Please read it before your appointment. Write down any questions and bring them with you.

What is a biopsy?

A biopsy is a small operation. We take a small piece from a patch, lump or sore in your mouth.

An expert then looks at it under a microscope. This tells us what the area is.

There are two main types:

  • Taking out the whole area. We do this for small lumps. You may not need any more treatment.
  • Taking out a small piece. This tells us what the area is, so we can plan the right treatment.

Why do I need a biopsy?

Looking at an area is not always enough to know what it is. A biopsy is the best way to find out.

Many people worry that a biopsy means cancer. Most biopsies show something that is not cancer. If you are worried, please tell us. We are happy to talk it through.

What are my choices?

  1. Have the biopsy. This is the best way to find out what the area is.
  2. Wait and check the area again later. This is only safe for some patches. If the area needs treatment, waiting could delay it. Your surgeon will tell you if waiting is an option for you.

The choice is yours. You can change your mind at any time, even after you have signed the form to say yes.

Before your appointment

  • Tell us about all the medicines you take. This includes ones you buy from a shop.
  • Tell us if you take a medicine to thin your blood, such as warfarin, apixaban or rivaroxaban.
  • Tell us if you take a medicine for your bones, such as alendronic acid or denosumab. Tell us even if you have stopped it.
  • Keep taking your medicines unless we tell you to stop.
  • You can eat and drink as normal before a biopsy with a numbing injection.
  • A few biopsies are done while you are asleep, under a . If this is you, your letter will tell you what to do.
  • Have paracetamol or ibuprofen at home, if they are safe for you.

What happens on the day?

  1. We numb the area with an injection. It takes a couple of minutes to work.
  2. We take the sample. You should not feel pain, but you may feel pushing.
  3. Most of the time, we close the area with . These melt away by themselves in about 2 weeks.
  4. We put the sample in a pot and send it to the laboratory.

It all takes about 15 minutes. Most people can go back to work or school the same day.

After the biopsy

  1. On the day
    • Your mouth is numb for a few hours.
    • A little blood in your spit is normal.
  2. The next few days
    • A little soreness or swelling.
  3. About 2 weeks
    • The stitches melt away.
    • You get your result.
  • Until the numbness wears off, take care not to bite your lip, cheek or tongue.
  • Paracetamol or ibuprofen can help the soreness, if they are safe for you.
  • A biopsy from the tongue is often more sore than one from other parts of the mouth.

Looking after your mouth

  • On the day, do not rinse your mouth. Rinsing can start the bleeding again.
  • From the next day, rinse gently with warm salt water after meals, for about a week. Use 1 teaspoon of salt in a cup of warm water.
  • Keep brushing your teeth. Be gentle near the wound.
  • Eat soft food for a few days. Wait until the numbness has gone before you have hot drinks.
  • Try not to smoke for at least a few days. Smoking slows healing and makes infection more likely.
  • If it bleeds, roll up a clean, damp cloth or piece of gauze. Bite on it firmly for 20 minutes. Do not keep checking it.

Your results

We usually give you your result at an appointment about 2 weeks after the biopsy. If the result is urgent, we will contact you sooner.

Make sure you get your result

  • If you have not heard from us within 3 weeks, please phone us.
  • Do not assume that no news is good news.

What are the risks?

A biopsy is a small operation and serious problems are rare. There are no good numbers for each kind of mouth biopsy, so we have used words instead.

Some soreness and swelling for a few daysMost people
A small scar inside your mouthMost peopleYou may feel it with your tongue. It softens over time and usually cannot be seen.
Bleeding at homeUncommonBiting on gauze usually stops it.
InfectionUncommon
The result is not clear, so we need to take another sampleUncommon
Numbness or tingling near the area, such as the lip or tongueRareThis usually gets better over time.

When should I get help?

☎Phone us if:

  • bleeding does not stop after biting on gauze for 20 minutes, twice
  • pain or swelling gets worse after the third day
  • you feel hot and unwell, or the wound tastes or smells bad

!Go to A&E or call 999 if:

  • you find it hard to breathe or swallow
  • swelling spreads to your eye or down your neck
  • bleeding is heavy and will not stop
